Question 9 of 12

Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

Question: Write equations for two lines through the point (3, −2):
Parallel to 3x + 4y = 7
Perpendicular to 3x + 4y = 7
Progress
0 / 3 warmups
01   What is this asking?

Given one line, find two new lines that both pass through the same point — one running parallel (same direction) and one running perpendicular (crossing at 90°).

Parallel slope
Same slope as original. Same direction. Never cross.
Perpendicular slope
Negative reciprocal: flip the fraction, change the sign.

Negative reciprocal rule:

If slope = −3/4,   perpendicular slope = 4/3
If slope = 3,   perpendicular slope = −1/3

Then use point-slope form to build the equation: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Tip — see Q7 cheat sheet
Q7 has the full slope reference covering all forms, parallel, and perpendicular conditions. Check it if you need a refresher.
02   Warmup
extract slope from standard form, find reciprocal
The moves you need
Solve 3x + 4y = 7 for y. What is the slope? (numerator only — answer: −?)
Slope of parallel line to 3x+4y=7 is also −3/4. As decimal: −0.?
Negative reciprocal of −3/4 is 4/3. Plug point (3,−2) and slope 4/3 into y−y₁=m(x−x₁). What is b (y-intercept)?
Those three answers are the slope of the original, slope of parallel, and y-intercept of perpendicular. You have all the pieces.
03   Cheat sheet

Parallel & Perpendicular
— 4 steps
Step 1
Get the original line into y = mx + b form. Read off slope m.
Step 2
Parallel: same slope m.   Perpendicular: slope = −1/m (flip fraction, change sign).
Step 3
Use point-slope form with your new slope and the given point (x₁, y₁):
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
Step 4
Solve for y to get slope-intercept form y = mx + b.
Check: plug the given point into your final equation — it must satisfy it.
04   Solve it
both parts, step by step
1
Get slope from 3x + 4y = 7
Solve for y:
4y = −3x + 7
y = (−3/4)x + 7/4
Original slope: m = −3/4
Part A
Parallel line through (3, −2)
2
Parallel slope = same slope
m_parallel = −3/4
3
Point-slope form with (3, −2)
y − (−2) = (−3/4)(x − 3)
y + 2 = (−3/4)x + 9/4
y = (−3/4)x + 9/4 − 2
y = (−3/4)x + 9/4 − 8/4
y = (−3/4)x + 1/4
Answer — Part A (Parallel)
y = −(3/4)x + 1/4
Same slope −3/4
Passes through (3,−2) ✓
Part B
Perpendicular line through (3, −2)
4
Perpendicular slope = negative reciprocal of −3/4
Flip the fraction: 3/4 → 4/3. Change the sign: −3/4 → +4/3.
m_perp = 4/3
5
Point-slope form with (3, −2)
y − (−2) = (4/3)(x − 3)
y + 2 = (4/3)x − 4
y = (4/3)x − 4 − 2
y = (4/3)x − 6
Answer — Part B (Perpendicular)
y = (4/3)x − 6
Slope 4/3 (neg. reciprocal of −3/4)
Passes through (3,−2) ✓
Check: (4/3)(3)−6 = 4−6 = −2 ✓
Verify perpendicular: slopes multiply to −1
(−3/4) × (4/3) = −12/12 = −1 ✓   That's how you confirm two lines are perpendicular.
