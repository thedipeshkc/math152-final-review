Question 10 of 12

Slope & Equation from a Graph

Question: A line passes through the points (0, −2) and (1, −4). Find the slope, y-intercept, and write the equation in slope-intercept form.
Progress
0 / 3 warmups
01   What is this asking?

You're given two points on a line. From those two points you can find everything about the line — its steepness, where it crosses the y-axis, and its full equation.

Step 1 — Slope
Use the slope formula: m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁). Rise over run.
Step 2 — Y-intercept
One point is (0, −2). When x = 0, that IS the y-intercept. b = −2.
Step 3 — Equation
Plug m and b into y = mx + b.

Shortcut for this question: One of the two points given is (0, −2). Whenever one point has x = 0, that point IS the y-intercept — no extra work needed!

02   Warmup
slope formula practice with these exact points
Points: (0, −2) and (1, −4)
y₂ − y₁ = −4 − (−2) = −4 + 2 = ?
x₂ − x₁ = 1 − 0 = ?
slope m = rise/run = −2/1 = ?
Rise is −2 and run is 1. The line drops 2 units for every 1 step right — that's a steep negative slope. Now we have everything to write the equation.
03   Cheat sheet
find equation of any line from two points
Line from Two Points
— slope, intercept, equation
Step 1
Slope formula: m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁). Label your points, subtract y's on top, subtract x's on bottom. Same order both times.
Step 2
Y-intercept: If one point has x = 0, that point's y-value IS b. Otherwise use point-slope form: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) and solve for b.
Step 3
Write the equation: y = mx + b. Plug in your m and b.
Verify
Plug both original points into your equation. Both should give true statements.
Negative slope = line falls from left to right.
Positive slope = line rises from left to right.
Steep line = large |m|.   Flat line = m close to 0.
04   Solve it
step by step from two points to full equation
1
Label the two points
(x₁, y₁) = (0, −2)    (x₂, y₂) = (1, −4)
2
Calculate the slope
m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
m = (−4 − (−2)) / (1 − 0)
m = (−4 + 2) / 1 = −2 / 1 = −2
Negative slope — the line falls as x increases.
3
Find the y-intercept
One point is (0, −2). When x = 0, the y-value IS the y-intercept:
b = −2   →   y-intercept = (0, −2)
No extra work needed — (0, −2) handed it to us.
4
Write slope-intercept form
y = mx + b
y = −2x + (−2)
y = −2x − 2
5
Verify with both points
Plug in (0, −2):
y = −2(0) − 2 = 0 − 2 = −2 ✓
Plug in (1, −4):
y = −2(1) − 2 = −2 − 2 = −4 ✓
Final Answer
y = −2x − 2
Slope = −2
Y-intercept = (0, −2)
Both points verified ✓
Why the y-intercept was free here
One of the given points was (0, −2). The y-intercept is ALWAYS the point where x = 0. So whenever a problem gives you a point with x = 0, that's your b — don't do any extra algebra. It's already done.
